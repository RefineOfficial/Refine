Buying order for a beginner

If you own nothing, buy in this order and stop when the budget runs out. The first three do most of the work.

    Total for the first three, in most countries, is less than one bottle of a "premium" serum. Price and effectiveness stopped correlating somewhere around the second shelf.

    One rule that prevents most damage

    Add one new product at a time, and give it two weeks before adding the next. When six things go on your face at once and your skin reacts, you've learned nothing about which one did it.

    Style

    Fit first, then colour, then everything else

    A cheap shirt that fits reads better than an expensive one that doesn't. Start with the fit checklist below, then pick a direction that suits the life you actually live — not the one in the reference photos.

    Six directions worth knowing

    None is better than another. The useful question is which one matches your climate, your job and the amount of attention you want.

    The fit checklist

    Stand in front of a mirror with what you're wearing now and go down the list. Anything unchecked is worth a tailor or a different size before it's worth a new purchase.

    Checks are saved in this browser so you can come back to it.

    Colour, simplified

    You don't need a season analysis. Two rules cover most situations.

    • Contrast: match the contrast of your outfit to the contrast between your hair and skin. High contrast suits crisp pairings; low contrast suits tonal ones.
    • Anchors: keep three quiet colours in rotation — one dark, one mid, one light. Add one colour you like. That's a wardrobe.

        When this stops being useful

        Self-improvement is supposed to cost you a little time and give you back some confidence. If the trade has flipped, that's worth taking seriously.

        • You're checking mirrors, photos or lighting so often it eats into your day
        • You're skipping meals, plans or people because of how you think you look
        • A missed day feels like a moral failure rather than a missed day
        • The list of things you'd need to fix keeps growing no matter what you do

        Any of those is a reason to talk to a doctor or a licensed therapist. It's a common pattern, it's treatable, and it isn't a character flaw.

        Hard lines

        A short list of things that circulate as advice and shouldn't. Not because they're edgy — because people get hurt.

        • Striking your own face or jaw to "stimulate bone growth". It causes fractures, nerve damage and asymmetry. There is no mechanism by which it works.
        • Prescription-only medication bought from a grey-market seller, at doses copied from a forum post
        • Aggressive restriction, fasting stacked on fasting, or any plan that treats hunger as proof it's working
        • DIY dermarolling, chemical peels or injectables at home
        • Any procedure marketed to under-18s, and any adult who encourages one
